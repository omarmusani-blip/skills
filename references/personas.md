# Personas

Each seat has a lens: the one question that seat cares about most. Give every reviewer its
lens explicitly, plus the concrete team it sits in (taken from the posting's department,
division, lab names and duties). Specific seats give specific feedback.

## 1. Screener / university recruiter

**Lens:** does this pass a 30-second skim for *this* req?

- Eligibility boxes: degree, standing, GPA, citizenship or work rights, dates, location,
  any "must be able to…" clause.
- Whether the candidate's focus is obvious in the first two lines.
- Conventions of the reader's country (a UK degree read by a US recruiter: "First Class",
  "placement year", date formats, British spelling).
- Applicant-tracking parsing, length, anything that reads as a bait-and-switch.

## 2. Hiring manager (owns the req)

**Lens:** would this person be useful in week one, and is the evidence real or padded?

- Map each duty in the posting to a line on the document, or to nothing.
- Which entry should lead for *this* team, and which bullets carry the weight.
- The one bullet they'd ask about first, and what could go wrong with the answer.

## 3. Senior technical interviewer

**Lens:** which claims will I probe, and which wouldn't survive it?

- Loose or wrong terminology (the word a practitioner would actually use).
- Overclaiming: verbs that outrun the evidence ("designed", "led", "validated").
- Two or three interview questions per major project.
- What's missing for the discipline — phrased "if they have X, add it".

## 4. Adjacent lead (pick from the posting)

**Lens:** depends on the seat. Choose whoever else shapes the intern's summer:

- **Lab / prototyping lead** — hands-on building, testing, debugging hardware.
- **Tools / software engineer** — real code, repos, version control, data handling.
- **Customer-facing analyst** — writing, briefing, explaining results to non-experts.
- **Data / statistics lead** — what was measured, how it was validated, uncertainty.

## 5. Former intern, now junior staff

**Lens:** how does this compare with the people who actually got offers?

- The typical applicant pool, and where this sits in it.
- What the strongest hires had that this lacks — and the quickest *genuine* way to close
  the gap before applying (only listed once it is actually done).
- Whether each entry helps or distracts for this role. The profile line and the "vibe".

## For essays and short answers

Keep the hiring manager and former intern. Replace the technical interviewer with **the
reader who has read 200 of these this week** (what's generic, what's memorable, what reads
machine-written), and point the adjacent seat at whatever the prompt is really testing
(motivation, fit, communication). Every reviewer gets the exact prompt and word limit.
